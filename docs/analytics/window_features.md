# Window Features

`mmla ses-fuse` turns a session's events (what the ASR, IPS and VFA pipelines stored in InfluxDB, see the [Database Reference](../database.md#influxdb)) into one table, the fusion table, one row per window (10 s by default), with the speech, space, body, gaze and action features side by side and a coverage count per modality, so that interaction detection, modality ablations and human codings can be joined on the window:

```bash
mmla ses-fuse -c pipelines/vfa-base/config.yml -sid <session-id>                       # from InfluxDB, through any config with an InfluxDB section
mmla ses-fuse --measurements artifacts/<session-id>/measurements -w 10 -st 10 -o features.csv   # from an export
```

Persons are tag ids (`-tags 0,1` names them, and they are then the session's pupils too; else the tags the events hold), pairs are sorted tag pairs; persons the pose model saw without a tag count only in the seat trace. A cell is empty when its modality contributed nothing to the window. The table lands in `artifacts/<session>/analysis/features/` with an analysis record next to it saying which events it came from; the export folder's `<session>_features.json` is the `vfa_features` events. Before any window is cut, the fusion remakes the gaze targets with the current [hand circle](#the-hand-circle-remade), labels each camera's [work area](#the-work-area) and carries the tags [along the tracks](#tags-along-the-tracks), in that order.

## Tags along the tracks

The features endpoint keeps a read tag on its track from then on (`tag_match: track`, see [Pose](../pipelines/vfa/index.md#pose)), but not backwards, and not once the tag was read on another track. Before any window is cut, the fusion carries the tags over the whole session, per camera: a tracked person without a tag, on a track that read a tag at some time, takes the tag of that track's nearest read (the earlier one on a tie) and is marked `tag_match: propagated`, with `person_id` the tag and the frame's pairs and gaze targets renamed to it. So the frames before a track's first read are named, and a track that read two tags is split between them where each untagged frame is nearer to one read or the other. A read tag, and a tag the server carried, are never changed. A tag is not given in a frame where another person already carries it, nor to two tracks in one frame (neither gets it), and a track id that appears twice in one frame names nobody. A track id not seen for more than 60 s (`TRACK_GAP_SECONDS`) counts as a new track, because ByteTrack keeps a lost track for 30 frames (30 s at one frame set a second) and a longer absence means the id was handed out again. A propagated person counts like a tagged one in every column, and the seat trace counts only the bodies still untagged after it; the seats themselves are learned without the propagated boxes, so a track carried to the wrong person does not move them. A track that never read a tag stays untagged. `window_features(..., track_tags=False)` builds the table without it; the table is then the same as before, and so is one from events without `track_id`.

## Pupils

A partner is another pupil of the session. The pupils are the `-tags` given to `mmla ses-fuse`, else the `pupils` the session's `manifest.json` declares (`mmla ses-tidy --pupils`), else the tags up to 12, the IPS trust bound (a higher one, such as 264, is a misread badge). The command prints them with their source and writes them into the analysis record (`pupils`, `pupils_source`). A gaze target is in the group when its `person_id` is a pupil's tag after the tags were carried along the tracks, so a pupil the server knew only as `track_8` counts once the fusion named the track. A target that stays untagged after that is still a pupil when its box centre stands at the seat (on that camera, the seat of the seat trace above) of a pupil whose tag the frame does not hold: the badge was not read, and nobody else sits there. On the 19 unlabelled sessions the rule moves 1,624 of the pupils' 13,987 gaze frames on someone else (11.6 %) to a partner (31,045 to 32,669 partner frames), 20 to 32 % of them in the sessions with the most unread badges (2025-06-16 micro:bit 437 of 1,355, 2025-10-07 WeGrow 68 of 234, 2025-04-08 group 2 187 of 673, 2025-06-16 WeGrow 90 of 328, 2024-12-10 group 2 200 of 1,003) and none in four sessions; without it these frames went to other_* and the partner shares were short. What stays `other_*` is mostly other persons, but still includes a pupil with an unread badge away from their seat, so it is a lower bound of that error, not zero. `n_vfa_seat_partners` counts the gaze frames named by the seat, and `data_checks.json` sums them per session under `fusion_check` (`seat_partner_frames`). `window_features(..., seat_partners=False)` leaves every untagged target as someone else. Who is someone outside the group for the joint split and the `nm_*` columns (a non-member) is ruled on separately, below.

## The work area

A gaze on the shared table, a microscope or a tablet lands on no face and no hand, and the server calls it `elsewhere`, together with a gaze at the floor or at the next table. Before any window is cut, the fusion learns, per camera of the session (and frame size), where the pupils' hands have been, and calls an `elsewhere` gaze that lands there `work_area` (`openmmla/services/vfa/work_area.py`, `label_work_areas`). The area is the box between the 2.5th and 97.5th percentile (nearest rank) of the pupils' hand centres on each axis, grown on every side by the median hand radius (the hand circles the server scores, 0.4 shoulder widths) and by the gaze tolerance every target gets (the longer side / 64), and clipped to the frame. It is ready from the 200th hand sighting (five sightings beyond each 2.5 % edge; about 40 to 60 s with two or three pupils at one frame set a second, 33 to 130 s on the median front camera). It is causal and cumulative: a frame is labelled with the hands of the frames up to and including it, never later ones, so the same frames in the same order give the same labels offline and online. It learns only from the tags the server gave (read or kept on the track), never the propagated ones, so a track carried to the wrong person cannot stretch it, and only from the pupils' hands, never the teacher's. Faces, hands, zones, `out_of_frame` and `unknown` keep priority: only `elsewhere` is relabelled. That matters, because in 2D the area is not free of faces (a partner's face above the table falls inside it: 2 to 82 % of partner-face gaze points on the front cameras and 84 to 99 % on the microscope cameras lie in the ready area), so the area works only because faces and hands are scored first. A frame that already carries `work_area` (a server that labels online) is kept as it is. On the 19 unlabelled sessions, 82.7 % of the pupils' `elsewhere` gaze frames became `work_area`, and the `elsewhere` share of seen gaze fell from 0.12-0.48 per session to 0.02-0.08 (0.21 in 2025-06-16 WeGrow). By eye on overlaid frames, the area is the table with its tray, microscope and tablet, and what stays `elsewhere` is the floor, the neighbouring table and other persons. Weak spots: the cramped 2025-06-16 WeGrow side camera relabels 46 % of its `elsewhere` (the other group's microscope correctly stays out); cameras on the edge of the group (2026-03-02 micro:bit c920-02 and c920-03, 2026-05-20 micro:bit c920-03, and c920-02 in 2025-10-07 and 2025-11-25 micro:bit, which see no pupil hands) are never or rarely ready, so their `p<tag>_work_area_ready_ratio` is low and the layout masks the share; and the microscope cameras' areas cover 0.30 to 0.73 of the frame. Padding by the hand radius matters on the microscope side cameras, where the eyepieces sit just beyond the hands on the focus knobs (2026-03-02 WeGrow c920-06: 70 % relabelled without it, 90 % with it). Early areas are smaller, which errs toward fewer relabels.

## The hand circle, remade

Every `vfa_features` event stored before the server's image was rebuilt for version 2 was made with version 1 of the [hand circle](../pipelines/vfa/index.md#pose) (0.14 shoulder widths past the wrist), and version 2 (0.33) moves which hands a gaze lands on and how far apart two persons' hands are. Before anything else, the fusion makes every frame's gaze targets and pair hand distances again from what the frame stores: the keypoints and boxes, `face_bbox`, the gaze's point and `inout`, the zones, the frame's size, and the thresholds the frame states in `scoring`, else the server's defaults, a `keypoint_confidence` of 0.3 and an `inout_threshold` of 0.5, which every replay used (`relabel_hand_circles`, with `features.relabel_answer` per frame). No video is replayed. It runs on the frames as the server named them, so it comes before the tags are carried along the tracks, and before the work area, which relabels only `elsewhere` gazes and learns from the same circles. The rest of the frame is kept, the gaze distances too. A frame that cannot be remade (no size, a person without keypoints, a box or a name), a frame that already carries `work_area` and a frame the server already made with the circle asked for (its `scoring` says so) stay as stored, as does the target of a person with a gaze point but no face box. With the version 1 circle the remake gives back the server's answers: over the 20 replayed sessions (132,772 frames), 652,136 of 652,299 gaze targets (99.975 %) and 1,634,879 of 1,636,243 pair hand distances within 0.5 px (99.92 %; 71 % to the 0.1 px). Every one of the other 163 targets and 1,364 distances is reproduced when the stored numbers are moved within their rounding (coordinates ±0.05 px, confidences and `inout` ±0.0005, 200 draws each); among them, 33 targets and 1,305 distances have a keypoint whose stored confidence rounds onto 0.3, and 33 targets an `inout` that rounds onto 0.5. With version 2, 44,094 targets (6.8 %, the rounding residue included) change: 30,206 from `elsewhere` to a person's own hands (16,093) or a partner's (14,113), 9,859 from one person's hands to another's, and 3,986 from hands to `elsewhere`; a pair's hand distance moves by a median −1.3 px (p10 −36, p90 +31). Per window, a pupil's share of gaze on their own hands rises by 0.038 on average and on a partner's by 0.021, and the work-area share falls by 0.061, since the work area only takes what the hands leave `elsewhere`; the work area of the 38 cameras grows by a median 2.6 % (p10 −3 %, p90 +7 %). `mmla ses-fuse -hr false` (`window_features(..., hand_relabel=False)`) keeps the stored targets and distances, and the work area and the hand columns then read the circle the frames were made with (version 1 for a frame without `scoring`), so for the replays every column the table had before the change keeps its value (checked cell by cell on three sessions); the analysis record says which circle a table used (`hand_circle`: `version`, `relabelled`, `nudge_radii`, `radius_shoulders`, `stored_frames`, the frames by the circle the server made them with, and the thresholds that stand in for a frame that states none).

## The hands in body units

The wrist speed and hand distances above are in frame widths, so a pupil nearer the camera, or a camera standing nearer the group or with a narrower field of view, moves faster and further. The pixels themselves cancel out: at the median seat a shoulder width is 0.100 of the frame's width at 540p micro:bit and 0.095 at 1080p micro:bit (the same view in twice the pixels), but 0.133 on the nearer 1080p microscope cameras. The columns added on 2026-09-24 measure the hands in body units. Each pupil's wrists are followed in the image, and each move is measured in the pupil's own shoulder widths (the mean of the two frames'), from one frame set to the next on one camera, reading only wrists and shoulders seen with confidence 0.5 or more (at least one wrist is seen so in 94 to 98 % of the pupils' frames by setup, both shoulders in 94 to 96 %). A step is two frame sets in a row, 0.75 to 1.5 s apart, that both hold the pupil, and only one body with the pupil's tag, on the same camera: in a frame set where two bodies wear one tag (a track's remembered tag and a read one) the step cannot tell which body it follows. The frame sets are 1.0 s apart in every replay (56,270 gaps), and the bounds below are moves in a 1 s step, so a session at another cadence (a `keyframe_interval` of 0.5) has no hand steps, and empty hand columns, until the bounds are measured for it. A step is **active** when a wrist moved at least 0.16 shoulder widths, **still** when every wrist seen moved less than 0.08, and neither in between. The two bounds come from the unlabelled replays, on the steps as the table takes them: a wrist whose arm stayed still in the image (its own elbow and shoulder each moved under 0.05 shoulder widths; nothing is subtracted, and the wrist itself is not in the definition, so its spread is not cut) moved a median 0.024 to 0.027, p90 0.078 to 0.084, p95 0.109 to 0.118 and p99 0.22 to 0.24 shoulder widths in a step, by setup (100,487 wrists). Still is under that p90, and active is twice the still bound, as the bounds were set when the wrists were followed against the shoulders (one step). That p90 is a convention of the definition, not the keypoints' noise alone. It follows the arm's bound (0.059 under 0.03, 0.110 under 0.08), since a looser arm lets real movement in, and one still step takes in arms that are just stopping or about to move: those whose previous or next step moved give a p90 of 0.089 to 0.098, while arms still on the previous and the next step as well give 0.055 to 0.061 (44,224 wrists), so that 0.08 is about their p95 and 0.16 above their p99 (0.13 to 0.16). Bounds of 0.06 and 0.12 would read 8 points more steps active (46.5, 43.6 and 54.0 % against 38.4, 35.9 and 45.9 % at 540p micro:bit, 1080p micro:bit and 1080p microscope) and 8 to 9 fewer still, and two cameras agree a little less there (κ 0.24, 0.33 and 0.30 against 0.26, 0.36 and 0.33; below), so reliability does not settle the choice, and the definition fixed first was kept. A bound on the wrist itself would decide its own p90 (under 0.10 its p99 is 0.097), while one far above it changes little (under 0.30 the p90 is 0.078 to 0.081). A step takes the largest of its wrists' moves, so a step whose two arms stayed still reads still in 85 to 90 % of cases and active in 1.8 to 2.7 %. A hand's length (wrist to farthest fingertip) is a median 0.46 shoulder widths (IQR 0.37 to 0.55; 0.48, 0.47 and 0.40 at 540p micro:bit, 1080p micro:bit and 1080p microscope), from the confident hands of the whole-body model the [hand circle](../pipelines/vfa/index.md#pose) was placed with (DWPose-l; 368 hands, mean score 0.7 or more); in hand lengths, still is under 0.17 and active from 0.35. Over every pupil step, 36 to 46 % read active, 35 to 45 % still and 19 to 20 % neither, by setup (the microscope sessions the most active).

**In the image, not against the shoulders.** Until the review of 2026-09-24 the wrists were followed against the midpoint of the shoulders, with the same bounds (a still arm's p90 was 0.077 to 0.081 that way), and shoulders moving over hands that stayed put moved them: on the replays the shoulder midpoint itself moved 0.16 shoulder widths or more in 32 to 48 % of the steps active that way, so a pupil who leaned in or turned with the hands resting on the table read as moving hands. In the image such a lean moves no wrist. Of the steps active against the shoulders, 2.9 to 4.3 % are still in the image and 11 to 12 % in between; of those in which the shoulders moved 0.16 or more, 6 to 7 % are still, 10 to 11 % in between and 83 % stay active, since hands mostly move with a body that moves. That is the image's own cost: hands carried along with the body (a pupil shifting in the chair with the hands in the lap) move, and in 30 to 46 % of the active steps the shoulders moved 0.16 or more as well. An active step is the hands moving, whether the arm or the body moved them, not work on the artifact. The share of steps active fell from 39 to 50 % to 36 to 46 %, and the share still rose from 28 to 39 % to 35 to 45 %; per window, the active shares of the two ways correlate at r 0.90 to 0.94, and two cameras agree a little better in the image (below).

Per person: `_hand_steps` (steps with a status), `_hands_active_ratio` and `_hands_still_ratio` (of those), `_wrist_speed_sw` (the mean wrist speed, shoulder widths a second) and `_other_hands_near_ratio`: of the person's frames with a hand circle and a shoulder width, the share in which a hand circle of a body outside the group has its centre within one hand length (0.46 of the person's shoulder width) of one of theirs. A body counts as the group's own, not as someone else, when it wears a pupil's tag; when its box overlaps a pupil's by an IoU of 0.5 or more or lies 60 % or more inside it (a second skeleton, or an arm seen apart); when it has a hand centred within 0.1 shoulder widths of a pupil's (a shared wrist); when its box centre stands at a pupil's seat on that camera, the pupil seen there or not; or when it is on a track the server gave a pupil's tag to at some time of the session. Of the bodies the first version of this column counted (with the IoU and the missing pupil's seat alone), these rules take out 30 % at 540p micro:bit, 53 % at 1080p micro:bit and 78 % at 1080p microscope, and the windows with a value above 0 fall from 15.5, 12.8 and 25.1 % to 12.2, 7.1 and 8.3 % of those with a value. What stays is not identified, since no frame is looked at: 53 to 77 % of the remaining runs of hits on a camera last one frame set, and 10 to 30 % of the remaining bodies are fragments with six keypoints or fewer, so a hit can still be a stray skeleton rather than the teacher or another group.

Per pair, over the steps in which both had a status on the same camera (`_hand_steps`): `_one_active_ratio` (one active, the other still), `_both_active_ratio`, `_both_still_ratio`, and `_follow_ratio`: of the one-active steps, given at least three, the share in which the still pupil's gaze at the step's end is on the active one's hands (their `partner_hands` target, or a gaze point in frame within reach of one of the active one's hand circles, the gaze tolerance included, unless the server found the still pupil's own hands nearer). A share of one or two steps could only be 0, 0.5 or 1, and 37 to 48 % of the pair windows with a one-active step had no more than two. The share is not raised by the activity: over all one-active steps the still pupil's gaze is on the active one's hands in 19.7, 18.0 and 9.6 % of them (540p micro:bit, 1080p micro:bit, 1080p microscope), and when both are still a pupil's gaze is on the other's hands, by the same test, in 26.4, 19.7 and 14.2 %; 10 to 16 % of the follows come from the gaze point alone. `_follow_ratio` says how much a still pupil's gaze was on a working partner's hands, the codebook's following, not that the work drew it; the pupil's gaze on a partner's hands over the whole window is `_gaze_partner_hands_ratio`. Over the frames that hold both with a hand distance and both shoulder widths: `_hand_dist_sw_min` and `_hand_dist_sw_mean`, the hand distance over the mean of the two shoulder widths, and `_hands_close_ratio`, the share of those frames with the hands within one hand length. A column with nothing to judge is empty, never 0. Every step, like the wrist speed, is taken on one camera and the cameras are then pooled, each step counting once.

On the 20 replayed sessions, a pupil seen in a window has the person columns in 83 to 99 % of such windows and a co-visible pair the step shares in 76 to 97 % (the microscope sessions lowest), `_follow_ratio` in 32 to 51 % (it needs three one-active steps). None is near-constant: `_hands_active_ratio` has a median of 0.33 to 0.47 by setup, `_one_active_ratio` 0.22, `_hand_dist_sw_min` 0.47 to 0.89; the sparsest are `_other_hands_near_ratio` (above 0 in 7 to 12 % of windows), `_follow_ratio` (0 in 52 to 74 %) and `_hands_close_ratio` (0 in 51 to 70 %). Where two cameras saw the same pupil on the same step, their statuses agree in 53 to 59 % of steps (Cohen's κ 0.26 at 540p micro:bit, 0.36 at 1080p micro:bit, 0.33 at 1080p microscope, against 0.25, 0.33 and 0.32 when the wrists were followed against the shoulders; 8,569 to 34,967 steps) and on active against not active in 71 to 75 % (κ 0.38 to 0.47, against 0.36 to 0.45); in 13 to 16 % of them one camera says still and the other active, and the two cameras' moves correlate at a Spearman ρ of 0.46 to 0.57: a movement along one camera's line of sight is short in that camera's image, and each camera hides different wrists. No choice of bounds does much better: from 0.05 and 0.10 to 0.15 and 0.30 shoulder widths, κ stays between 0.23 and 0.38. Over a window, where both cameras judged five or more of the same steps, their active shares correlate at r 0.54 to 0.63 (0.55 to 0.63 against the shoulders) and their still shares at 0.45 to 0.57 (0.41 to 0.57). The window's shares pool about nine steps a camera and are a noisy reading of a pupil's hands.

Limits. These are 2D positions from the pose model's wrists: hands close together are not hands touching, a hand at rest on the artifact can be working (fine manipulation with a still wrist reads still), hands carried along by a moving body read as movement (a lean over resting hands does not), and nothing here says what a hand holds. At one frame set a second a handover or a pointing stroke is seen once or not at all. Two cameras on one pupil agree only fairly on a step (above). The group's own bodies are told from others by boxes, seats and tracks: for `_other_hands_near_ratio` a teacher standing at a pupil's seat counts as the group, a stray skeleton away from the seats as someone else. For the joint split and `nm_*` (below) a teacher at the seat of a pupil in the frame is a non-member, and only a body at the seat of a pupil missing from the frame is taken for that pupil, so a teacher standing where an absent pupil sat still counts as the group. The whole-body model's hand lengths and centroids come from 128 sampled frames and are an agreement between two models, not ground truth.

## Joint attention against its own past

Pupils who sit close look at the same table a lot, so the raw share of close gaze points partly measures seating. `pair<a>_<b>_joint_attention_baseline` compares each gaze point of a in the window (on a camera, in frame, at time t) with b's gaze point on the same camera at the frame set nearest t − d, within 0.5 s, for d = 20, 30 and 40 s, and the same with a and b swapped: a hit when the two lie within 5 % of the frame's width. The baseline is hits over comparisons, pooled over the cameras, the lags and both directions, and empty below 10 comparisons; `_joint_attention_excess` is the window's `_joint_attention_ratio` minus it. The lags sit past the decay of a joint episode and still within the same seating and task phase: on the unlabelled sessions the share of close gaze points falls from 0.49 at the same moment to 0.32 at 20 s, 0.29 at 40 s and 0.20 at 300 s, so by 20 s it has made 84 % of its drop to the 40 s level. Only b's points before the window are compared, whatever the window's length (a 60 s window would otherwise meet itself 20 s back), so the baseline is causal in time, but not in who a gaze point belongs to: that comes from the tags carried along the tracks over the whole session, where a track takes the tag of its nearest read even when that read comes later, and from seats learned from the whole session. So a badge read at minute 20 can change the baseline (and the partner/other split, like every tagged column) of a window at minute 18, and a server that keeps tags on tracks only forward would not reproduce these values exactly. The work area is not affected: it learns only from the tags the server gave, in time order. A joint episode that began 20 to 40 s earlier is part of the baseline: the excess measures change against the pair's recent level. 97 % of the pair-windows with three or more joint frames reach 10 comparisons. The analysis record holds the constants (`work_area`, `joint_baseline`).

## Where joint attention met

Two gazes close together can be on the group's own table or on a teacher demonstrating at it, and the codebook calls the first interaction and pupils following a teacher individual work. Since 2026-09-29 the fusion splits the joint attention of every pupil pair by what the gazes met (`window_features(..., joint_split=True)`, `mmla ses-fuse -js true`; off by default, since its DEV validation did not pass the pre-registered criteria, so the default table is the one fused before it, byte for byte). First every frame's non-members are found: the bodies that wear no pupil's tag; are no second skeleton or part of a pupil (the tests of `_other_hands_near_ratio`: an IoU of 0.5, 60 % inside, a shared wrist); do not stand at the seat of a pupil missing from the frame, whatever tag they wear (a misread badge such as 264 at its owner's seat is the owner); are on no track that carried a pupil's tag within 60 s; and are at least 0.6 of the pupils' size, their shoulder width against the median shoulder width of the pupils in the frame (else of the pupils on that camera over the session), or with their shoulders unseen their box width against the pupils' median box width. A smaller body is a far one, at the next table, and neither the group nor a non-member. A frame whose camera never saw a pupil's shoulders cannot tell, and has no non-members. Unlike `_other_hands_near_ratio`, a body at the seat of a pupil who is in the frame is not excused: a teacher leaning in beside a pupil is exactly who is meant. The 0.6 is fixed from geometry, not labels: a body at the group's depth reads about 1, one about 1.7 times as far from the camera 0.6 or less. The bound does not assume an adult reads larger than the pupils: a teacher standing behind the table can read a little smaller, and still passes.

Each pupil's gaze then has a referent. A gaze on someone else's face or hands (`other_face`, `other_hands`) is on an outsider when its target is a non-member (or the frame cannot tell), on the group when the target is the group's own by the rules above, and on nothing when it is a far body. A gaze point within reach of a non-member, within the gaze tolerance of one of their hand circles or of their face box grown by 20 %, is on an outsider whatever its label: the server gives a point to the nearest hand, so a teacher's hand beside a pupil's can leave the gaze named `partner_hands` or `own_hands`. Otherwise a partner's face or hands, the pupil's own hands and the work area are the group's own, and `elsewhere` and zones are nothing. A joint frame (the two gazes within 5 % of the width) is an outsider frame when either gaze is on an outsider, since two points that close are on the same thing, and a member frame when both are on the group's own; the rest (met elsewhere, in a zone, or on a teacher the pose model did not see) stays in `_joint_attention_ratio` only. `_joint_member_baseline` uses the baseline's comparisons, a hit counting only when both gazes were on the group's own things, so it keeps the meaning of the pair's own rate of meeting at their table. There is no outsider baseline: a demonstration lasts minutes, the gaze 20 to 40 s earlier is on the same teacher, and a baseline would absorb exactly the episode the column should show.

`nm_at_table_ratio` judges on each camera's table box, its work area as the last frame of the session holds it (offline, like the seats, so the first 40 to 60 s of a session, before the area is ready, are judged too), and grows it by one pupil shoulder width (about two hand lengths, an adult leaning in) for the body's box. `nm_hands_in_table_ratio` counts only hands inside the box itself, someone outside the group handling the group's artifact, and is the more specific of the two: a body standing near the table, a passer-by or a teacher watching, also reaches the grown box. A teacher the pose model missed, or one the rules take for the group (at an absent pupil's seat, on a pupil's track, overlapping a pupil's box), reads 0, not empty. The analysis record holds the constants (`joint_split`: the member labels, the outsider rule, `non_members` with `scale` 0.6, `table_reach_sw` 1.0, the table box, the track gap and the seat rule).
